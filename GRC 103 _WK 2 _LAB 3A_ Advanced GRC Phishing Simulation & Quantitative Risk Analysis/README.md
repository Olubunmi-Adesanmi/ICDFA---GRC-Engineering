## 1. Project Overview

This project demonstrates how Governance, Risk and Compliance (GRC) professionals can assess, quantify and communicate human-related cybersecurity risks using phishing simulation results and financial risk analysis.

The assessment is based on a fictional organisational scenario involving GlobalTech Inc., which conducted three phishing simulations targeting employees, executives and assistants.

The project analyses employee responses to simulated phishing emails, calculates phishing susceptibility metrics, projects potential organisational exposure, evaluates financial risk and assesses the cost-effectiveness of proposed security controls.

The primary objective is to demonstrate how technical security findings and human-related vulnerabilities can be translated into measurable business risks to support executive decision-making and security investment planning.

## 2. Project Objectives

The assessment focuses on the following objectives:

- Calculate fundamental phishing metrics, including email open rates, click-through rates, credential-submission rates and additional data-entry rates.
- Evaluate attack-stage conversion rates to understand employee susceptibility.
- Project phishing results across the organisation's workforce.
- Quantify potential financial exposure using Single Loss Expectancy (SLE) and Annualized Loss Expectancy (ALE).
- Conduct statistical analysis using approximate 95% confidence intervals.
- Develop a risk assessment matrix to prioritise identified risks.
- Evaluate proposed security controls using cost-benefit analysis and Return on Investment (ROI).
- Develop executive-level recommendations supported by quantitative findings.

## 3. Scenario Background

GlobalTech Inc. conducted three phishing simulations during the previous quarter.

The campaigns were designed around realistic organisational communication scenarios:

## 4. Assessment Methodology

The assessment follows a structured quantitative risk analysis approach, divided into four phases.

### Phase 1: Basic Phishing Metrics

The first phase evaluates employee interactions with the simulated phishing emails.

Metrics calculated include:

- Email open rate.
- Click-through rate.
- Credential-submission rate.
- Additional data-entry rate.
- Open-to-click conversion rate.
- Post-click credential-submission conversion rate.

### Phase 2: Advanced Risk Quantification

The second phase translates the simulation results into projected organisational and financial exposure.

Calculations include:

- Projected credential submissions.
- Expected breach-equivalent outcomes.
- Single Loss Expectancy (SLE).
- Annualized Loss Expectancy (ALE) under the lab's simplified model.
- Modelled financial exposure per campaign.
- Employee and payroll context.

### Phase 3: Comprehensive Risk Assessment

The third phase evaluates the identified risks and their statistical uncertainty.

Activities include:

- Development of a campaign risk assessment matrix.
- Provisional risk classification.
- Identification of priority treatment actions.
- Calculation of approximate 95% confidence intervals.
- Interpretation of sample-size limitations.

### Phase 4: Executive Reporting and Control Economics

The fourth phase evaluates the proposed security investments and presents findings in business and financial terms.

Controls assessed include:

- Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA).
- Security awareness training.
- Email filtering.

The assessment also covers:

- Control implementation costs.
- Estimated risk-reduction benefits.
- Net financial benefits.
- Return on Investment (ROI).
- Proposed security investment portfolio.
- Break-even analysis.
- Executive recommendations.

## 6. Security Control Cost-Benefit Analysis

Three security controls were evaluated independently against the Campaign B modelled financial exposure.

| Security Control | Implementation Cost | Modelled Risk Reduction | ROI |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Multi-Factor Authentication | $90,000 | $651,847,500 | 724,175% |
| Security Awareness Training | $100,000 | $391,500,000 | 391,400% |
| Email Filtering | $50,000 | $130,500,000 | 260,900% |

The results indicate that MFA provides the highest modelled risk-reduction benefit and stand-alone ROI under the supplied assumptions.

The control benefits are independent scenarios and must not be added together without accounting for overlapping effects.

## 7. Risk Assessment and Prioritisation

The campaigns were assessed using credential-submission rates, modelled financial exposure and their respective target populations.

| Campaign | Provisional Risk Rating | Priority |
|---|---|---:|
| A – HR Bonus Notification | Medium-High | 2 |
| B – IT System Password Reset | Critical | 1 |
| C – CEO Urgent Document Review | High | 3 |

Campaign B requires immediate attention through stronger identity controls, improved password-reset processes, email protection and targeted employee training.

## 8. Key Recommendations

The assessment proposes the following security improvements:

1. Implement enterprise-wide MFA, prioritising privileged and high-risk accounts.
2. Strengthen password-reset and identity-verification procedures.
3. Conduct targeted phishing awareness training.
4. Improve email security through malicious URL detection, attachment scanning and impersonation protection.
5. Establish a consistent phishing reporting and incident triage process.
6. Conduct quarterly phishing simulations.
7. Monitor security control effectiveness using measurable KPIs.
8. Validate breach frequency and financial-loss assumptions before using projected ALE for actual budget forecasts.

## 9. Project Limitations

The assessment is based on a supplied training scenario and not on verified organisational incident data.

Important limitations include:

- The phishing simulation target groups may overlap.
- The sample does not necessarily represent the entire workforce.
- Projected credential submissions do not equal confirmed compromised accounts.
- Expected breach-equivalent outcomes do not automatically represent distinct breach incidents.
- The $652.5 million figure relies on a simplified annual loss assumption.
- Email-filtering effectiveness is an assumed planning input.
- The proposed 90% portfolio risk reduction is a management target, not a measured outcome.
- The supplied industry comparator figures were not independently verified.

These limitations are documented to distinguish observed results from assumptions and modelled projections.
